Mailman Delivers Defeat to Local Beatbox Founder in Garage Showdown

Eleven-person crowd witnesses stunning upset as postal carrier out-loops Association founder Joe Perg in his own garage

By a Staff Correspondent Exhibition Battle Report

DUBUQUE, IOWA — A folding-chair crowd of eleven watched the German Beatboxing Association's founder suffer his first clean loss in Association history on Tuesday night, as a local mail carrier known only as "the Mailman" out-paced, out-layered, and ultimately out-lasted Joe Perg in an exhibition battle held in Perg's own garage.

The bout, billed informally as a grudge match dating back to Perg's toddler years, opened with the founder at full force. Witnesses described a rapid-fire kick-snare pattern layered with a vocal scratch that "sounded like a record getting dragged backward," according to one attendee. Perg closed his opening round by pointing directly at his opponent and declaring, "You deliver bills, I deliver bars" — a line the crowd is still repeating.

"That's illegal." — Tiff Perg, from the merchandise table

The Mailman, who did not speak before, during, or after the battle, answered with what several attendees called the most unsettling routine they had ever witnessed at a Association event: an idling-truck kick drum, a snare that landed, as one witness put it, "on every doorbell Joe's ever rung asking for a rematch," and a closing vocal run combining a barking dog, a slamming screen door, and what several in attendance swore was a passable impression of a delivery slip being torn from a pad.

Perg attempted to answer in Round 2 with a display of his oft-cited ability to beatbox in 1,048 languages, cycling through what witnesses described as French, Spanish, and "something that might have been Finnish" before closing on a scream-whisper of "I. Have. A. Garage." The line drew laughter, but not, by most accounts, respect.

It was the Mailman's second-round answer that effectively ended the contest. Without breaking tempo, he reproduced, note for note, the rhythm of a mail truck's turn signal for eight consecutive bars — a routine so precise and so unbothered that the garage reportedly fell silent except for the beat itself.

"He's not even mad. That's the scariest part." — an unnamed member of the crowd

The final exchange was brief. Perg, by his own admission afterward, "dropped the beat" — a first in Association history — while the Mailman continued, unbothered, as though the exhibition had never been a contest to him at all. The Association's host declared the United States Postal Service the winner from atop a folding chair.

Reached for comment as he left the garage, the Mailman offered a single line: "I deliver Tuesdays and Thursdays." Perg, seated on an overturned bucket, requested a rematch at Nationals. The Association's board — Perg himself, in four of his five executive capacities — approved the request the following morning.
