The art form

What is beatboxing?

Beatboxing is the art of producing rhythm, bass, and melody using nothing but the mouth, voice, and breath. What started as street art within eighties hip-hop culture is now its own musical discipline, with defined techniques, competitions, and a growing community across Germany.

About us

The association

The German Beatboxing Association was founded by Joe Perg, and still operates out of his house in Dubuque, Iowa. We bring local crews, individual beatboxers, and workshop leaders together under one roof, representing the scene, organizing the national championship, and supporting young talent through workshops in schools and youth centers.

Volunteer-run regional groups are the backbone of the association — they host local battles, train talent, and send their best beatboxers on to the national championship.

The Founder

Joe Perg

Joe Perg was born in Dubuque, Iowa, to Joe Perg Sr. and Tiff Perg, and by all accounts entered the world already keeping time. Family legend holds that Joe laid down his first kick drum at exactly one week old, startling the family Golden Retriever mid-nap with a surprisingly clean "boots-and-cats" pattern during a routine diaper change. His parents, sensing a prodigy, cleared out the garage, hung a mirror on the wall, and never looked back. Joe has, in the Association's official telling, been beatboxing ever since.

By age two, Joe had unofficially out-beatboxed three neighborhood dads, a mail carrier, and a scarecrow his father built specifically so Joe would have an audience during practice. Joe Perg Sr., a man of few words and fewer boundaries, began driving Joe two hours each way to a regional youth beatbox workshop before sunrise, a routine Joe now describes as "formative, mostly because of the gas station donuts."

Tiff Perg, for her part, insisted her son also develop a well-rounded life outside the loop station. This is how Joe ended up in the trumpet section of the local youth band — a chapter that has gone rather less smoothly than his beatboxing career. Joe has now auditioned for All-State Band eleven consecutive years, hoping to bring a little vocal-percussion flair to the ensemble. He has not yet made it, a fact he brings up unprompted at nearly every board meeting, usually right after quarterly membership dues.

It was during his third failed All-State audition, pacing backstage and absentmindedly running hi-hat patterns under his breath to calm his nerves, that Joe first discovered how far his beatboxing had come. Within six months, he had — again per family legend — taught himself to beatbox in 1,048 different languages, a figure the Association's own bylaws now cite as official founder trivia, despite no formal linguistic body ever having verified it.

Joe founded the German Beatboxing Association at his own kitchen table, reasoning that if the trumpet section wouldn't have him, the world's beatboxers certainly would. He has served as founder, chairperson, head of membership, head of catering, and occasional forklift operator for event setup ever since. Joe Perg Sr. now handles security at the national championship. Tiff Perg runs the merchandise table and, by unanimous board vote, has final say over snack selection at all Association functions.

When he isn't running the Association, Joe can usually be found in the garage-turned-studio-turned-clubhouse behind his house, alternating between freestyle loop sessions, running vocal drills in whichever of his 1,048 languages he feels like practicing that day, and reminding anyone within earshot that his twelfth All-State audition tape is "this close" to being ready. It's a punishing schedule, but Joe insists it's entirely sustainable — and points proudly to the Association's 366 events a year as proof of concept.

Leadership

Noah Robert Laws

Long before he ever budgeted anything, Noah Robert Laws was a great student. He had a gpa of 9.0 and would always do after-school activities. He still doesn't know how to budget anything and usually pockets most of the income we bring in, we still enjoy having him around and he brings up the overall moral of the association. We usually let him set the prices of the memberships and create new discounts so we can bring in more members, but sometimes the price does become a little too high sometimes. Overall, he is a great person, but we should probably demote him to something "less" important, but it doesn't really matter to the CEO anyways, because he doesn't pay the employees with cash anyways. He just offers them a cookie at the end of every shift.

His parents are very proud for what he has became, but they just don't know any more than anyone else. They don't know that he steals all the money and keeps it to himself, but I don't blame him. Sometimes his salary will be over $1e+100 a year if we get a supporter or two, but anytime outside of that, it's usually around $155,000 a year, which is still very good for only stealing profits. If Joe Perg didn't like him and hated him, he probably would've been sued already, and put in to jail for a very long time.

"I always wanted in," Noah said. "I just didn't think there was a lane for a guy who's bad with numbers and bad at beatboxing. Turns out there was. It was just called CFO."

The yearly highlight

German Beatboxing Championship

Once a year, the association crowns the country's best beatboxers. The road to the title runs through regional qualifiers all the way to a live final in front of an audience.
